# 05 — Sales & Marketing Strategy

## 5.1 Target Customer Personas

### Persona A: College HOD (Primary Decision Maker)
- **Title:** Head of Computer Science Department
- **Pain Points:** Low placement %, outdated curriculum, student complaints about teaching quality, pressure from management
- **Wants:** Better placement numbers, industry exposure for students, accreditation points
- **Fear:** Students choosing other colleges with better industry connections
- **Decision Criteria:** Cost, quality of instructor, relevance to curriculum, references from other colleges
- **How to reach:** Email → Phone call → In-person meeting at college

### Persona B: College Principal / Director (Budget Approver)
- **Pain Points:** Budget constraints, needing to differentiate the college
- **Wants:** ROI on training spend, improved NIRF ranking, better placement stats
- **Decision Criteria:** Proven track record, references, institutional credibility
- **How to reach:** Through HOD recommendation, industry events

### Persona C: Student (B2C Sales)
- **Pain Points:** Can't crack coding interviews, don't know what industry expects, feel college isn't preparing them
- **Wants:** Clear path to a job, practical skills, confidence
- **Decision Criteria:** Price, peer recommendations, visible results (testimonials)
- **How to reach:** Instagram, LinkedIn, college WhatsApp groups, on-campus posters

## 5.2 Sales Process (B2B — College Partnerships)

### Step 1: Prospecting (10 hrs/week)
- Identify target colleges by tier (start with Tier 2)
- Find HOD email from college website
- Prepare personalized outreach mentioning their specific challenges
- Track all in a CRM (Google Sheets initially)

### Step 2: Initial Outreach
```
Subject: Help your students bridge the industry gap (BMS/CMRIT/DSCE reference)

Dear Prof. [Name],

I'm reaching out because most 3rd-year CS students at Bangalore colleges 
graduate knowing theory but not how to apply it in real software engineering 
roles.

I've spent [X] years as a Windows Senior Administrator / Software Engineer 
and have been teaching practical CS concepts to engineering students.

I recently worked with [Reference College] where students went from struggling 
with DBMS theory to designing production-ready schemas in 4 sessions.

Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week to discuss how this could 
help your students' placement outcomes?

Best,
Saqlain Hussain
SimpleLinks Edu
```

### Step 3: Free Guest Lecture (Close Rate: 40%+)
- Offer a 90-minute free session for a 3rd-year class
- Make it highly engaging, practical, and visibly different from regular teaching
- Collect student feedback immediately — share with HOD

### Step 4: Proposal & Pilot (Close Rate: 60%+ after guest lecture)
- Send a 2-page proposal with scope, pricing, timeline
- Offer a pilot module (4 sessions) at a discounted rate
- Include student testimonials from the guest lecture

### Step 5: Full Engagement
- Deliver pilot → measure impact → propose full semester engagement
- Ask for testimonial and referral to other HODs

## 5.4 Content Marketing Strategy

### LinkedIn (Your Primary Channel)
Post 3–4 times per week:

| Day | Content Type | Example |
|-----|-------------|---------|
| Monday | **Industry insight** | "What companies expect vs. what colleges teach in DBMS" |
| Wednesday | **Teaching snippet** | Video of you explaining a concept in 60 seconds |
| Friday | **Student story** | "One of my students just got placed at..." |
| Weekend | **Career advice** | "3 things I wish I knew as a CS student" |

### YouTube Channel
- Record 5–10 min concept explainers
- Title format: "DBMS in the Real World: Episode 1 — Why Your College Taught Normalization Wrong"
- Post weekly → build audience → direct to paid courses

### Student Ambassador Program
- Recruit 1–2 students per target college
- They share SimpleLinks content in college groups
- Get free access to all courses + ₹2K/month stipend
- Metrics: referrals generated, content shared

## 5.5 Sales Pipeline Management

| Stage | Definition | Target Time | Conversion |
|-------|-----------|-------------|------------|
| 1. Lead | Identified college/HOD with contact info | — | — |
| 2. Contacted | Personalized email sent | Week 1 | 30% reply rate |
| 3. Meeting | Phone/video call scheduled | Week 2 | 50% of replied |
| 4. Guest lecture | Free on-campus session delivered | Week 3–4 | 80% of meetings |
| 5. Proposal sent | Scope + pricing shared | Week 4 | 100% after guest lecture |
| 6. Pilot signed | Paid pilot engagement agreed | Week 5–6 | 40% of proposals |
| 7. Full partnership | Multi-subject annual deal | Semester 2 | 60% of pilots renew |

**Pipeline math for Year 1:**
- 50 colleges contacted → 15 replies → 8 meetings → 5 guest lectures → 2 pilots → 1 full partnership
- In parallel: run B2C bootcamps to generate cash flow while B2B sales cycle runs
